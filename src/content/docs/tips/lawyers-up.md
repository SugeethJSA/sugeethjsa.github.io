**Lawyer's Up** is a mock trial / debate event where participants argue a case before a judge, alternating between prosecution and defense. Unlike Shipwreck (which is about self-preservation), Lawyer's Up is about building a watertight legal argument.

> Some of these points were shared by Shashank anna, a senior with extensive Shipwreck and Lawyer's Up experience.

## The 18 Golden Rules

### Delivery & Presence

1. **Master Body Language** — Move around with energy and confidence. Your body language plays a crucial role in conveying your message effectively.

2. **Clarity is Key** — Clearly articulate your points to ensure your message is easily understood and leaves a lasting impact.

3. **Strategic Tone** — Use a confident and assertive tone, rather than being outright aggressive. Passive-aggressive tones undermine your professionalism.

4. **Charismatic Delivery** — Engage the audience through charismatic voice modulation, capturing their attention and reinforcing your argument's impact.

5. **Light-hearted Humor** — Inject tasteful humor to lighten the mood without detracting from the gravity of your argument.

### Argument Structure

6. **Structured Content** — Organize your content logically and coherently. A well-structured argument boosts your confidence and makes it easier for the audience to follow.

7. **Logical Precision** — Avoid overcomplicating your arguments with unnecessary complexity. Focus on clear, concise logic to make your case more compelling.

8. **Exploit Mistakes** — Capitalize on your opponent's errors, especially logical fallacies, to strengthen your position.

9. **Swift Rebuttal** — Prevent your opponent from gaining ground by responding promptly and thoughtfully to their points.

10. **Fluidity and Creativity** — If you face a mental block, weave engaging stories that relate to the topic to maintain the flow.

### Emotional & Ethical Appeal

11. **Emotional Connection** — Introduce emotions like empathy or urgency strategically to evoke a relatable connection with the audience.

12. **Empathy and Sympathy** — Elicit sympathy when applicable, highlighting the human aspect to foster a stronger connection.

13. **Quoting Authority** — Incorporate relevant quotes from experts or authoritative sources to lend weight to your argument.

14. **Showcase Your Perspective** — Subtly highlight your strengths and expertise without overshadowing the case. Do not aggressively aim to defame the opponent.

### Integrity & Professionalism

15. **Factual Integrity** — Base your arguments on accurate, provable facts. Misrepresentation or falsehoods severely damage your credibility.

16. **Precision in Language** — Exercise caution with your words, ensuring they align precisely with your intended message. Avoid irrelevant information.

17. **Consider Audience Comfort** — If uncomfortable with the topic, communicate your concerns to the judges professionally.

18. **Global Copyright Awareness** — Display a thorough understanding of international copyright laws (US, UK, India) when handling intellectual property matters.

> The goal of effective lawyering is to present a compelling, well-supported case while maintaining professionalism and integrity throughout the process.

## Practice Cases

### MacBook Gate

Mr. Whitman, a long-time Apple user, bought a MacBook Pro 2017 (Touch Bar) in 2018. The machine suffered from DisplayGate and Butterfly Keyboard issues. After the 'e' key failed, he sent an important email to his manager without noticing the missing character, was perceived as "offensive," and was terminated. He sues Apple for loss of trust, financial loss, and defamation.

**Prosecution** — Apple is liable for faulty design and inadequate public notice of the repair program.

**Defense** — Mr. Whitman should have checked Apple's website for the free repair program.

### AC Fire

Mr. Stario Matramak bought an O General AC. After a few years, the AC caught fire, causing property damage.

**Prosecution** — The AC was defective and the company is liable.
**Defense** — Lack of maintenance or improper usage caused the fire.

### Background Score Recreation

Mr. Kureshi, a YouTuber, recreated the background score of "Ennadi Maayavi Nee" for his videos.

**Prosecution** — Copyright infringement.
**Defense** — Fair use / transformative work.

### Publishing Books

Mr. Jayaraj published a book on Google Books. Google reviewed and removed it.

**Prosecution** — Google arbitrarily removed the book without proper justification.
**Defense** — Google has the right to enforce its content policies.

## Reference Material

For copyright-related cases, familiarize yourself with:

- **Indian Copyright Act, 1957** and **Copyright Rules, 2013**
- **The Berne Convention** for international copyright protection
- **Creative Commons** licenses and open-source licensing
- **Consumer Protection Laws** — relevant for product liability cases

Understanding these legal foundations makes your arguments more credible and grounded.
