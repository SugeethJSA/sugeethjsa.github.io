**Shipwreck** is a persuasion / debate event where participants argue why they (or their assigned character) deserves the last life jacket on a sinking ship. The judge decides who is most convincing.

## Types of Shipwreck

### Normal Shipwreck
Plead for yourself. Convince the judge why you, as yourself, deserve to survive.

### Anti-Shipwreck
Plead for your opponent. Argue why the other person deserves the life jacket more than you. Requires deflecting attention away from yourself.

### Situational Shipwreck
You are assigned a specific character and scenario. Argue from that character's perspective. Examples: celebrity on a sinking yacht, scientist with a vital discovery, etc.

## Judging Criteria

| Criterion | Description |
|---|---|
| **Spontaneity** | How natural and unrehearsed your argument feels |
| **Originality** | Creative and unexpected angles |
| **Language & Expression** | Clarity, vocabulary, and delivery |
| **Body Language** | Gestures, posture, eye contact |
| **Content** | Substance, logic, and emotional appeal |

> [!TIP]
> Humour is accepted and often rewarded when used appropriately.

## Winning Tips

1. **Body language matters** — stand tall, maintain eye contact, use purposeful gestures
2. **Speak clearly** — pace yourself, vary your tone, project your voice
3. **Avoid logical fallacies** — ad hominem, straw man, and false dilemmas weaken your case
4. **Use humour strategically** — a well-timed joke can win the crowd
5. **Charisma counts** — be likable, be genuine, connect with the judge
6. **Mix emotion with facts** — pure logic feels cold; pure emotion feels manipulative
7. **Quote authority** — reference a book, a leader, or a proverb to add weight
8. **Adapt on the fly** — respond to what your opponent says; don't read from a script

## Sample Scenarios

- **Normal**: You are a single mother with a young child visiting a sick relative
- **Anti**: A young off-duty lifeguard vs. an elderly woman who raised five children
- **Situational**: A controversial celebrity on a sinking yacht with their assistant
