Back in Class 9, my friend Anurag and I were appointed as Election Commissioners for a class election. It wasn't a real election — just an activity — but we took it seriously.

## The Setup

Two classes (9 C and 9 D) were holding a joint election. Nominations, campaigning, voting — the whole democratic process, compressed into a week. Our job was to oversee everything: candidate registration, debate moderation, ballot counting, and dispute resolution.

## The JECC

We formed the **Joint Election Commission** (JECC) — a name that sounded far more official than we were. We drafted rules, set deadlines, and prepared documentation.

## Key Responsibilities

- **Voter registration** — ensuring every student was listed
- **Candidate vetting** — verifying nominations met the criteria
- **Debate moderation** — keeping the discussion civil and on-topic
- **Ballot design and counting** — secret ballot, manual count
- **Results declaration** — the big moment

## What I Learned

Running an election — even a mock one — teaches you a lot about fairness, transparency, and the importance of clear rules. We had disputes, accusations of bias, and last-minute rule challenges. Sound familiar?

It was one of my best projects in school. Not because it was flashy, but because it mattered to the people involved.
