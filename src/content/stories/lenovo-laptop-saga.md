It started with a touchpad that stopped working. Then a wire that shorted. Then a sub-board that burnt out — literally, a fire hazard inside my laptop.

This is the story of my Lenovo laptop and the four-year repair odyssey that followed.

## The First Signs

The touchpad began acting erratically. Cursor jumps. Unregistered clicks. Then one day — nothing. Dead. I switched to an external mouse and carried on. Life goes on.

## The Short

When I finally opened it up, I found the problem: a touchpad wire had shorted against the motherboard. The insulation had worn through, probably from years of opening and closing the lid. A simple fix in theory — but the damage was already spreading.

## The Fire Hazard

A few weeks later, the sub-board burnt out. Actual burn marks on the board. The laptop was now a literal fire risk. I stopped using it on my bed.

The repair shop quoted me more than the laptop was worth. I declined.

## Four Years Pass

I used it sparingly — external keyboard, external mouse, never moving it. The chassis held together by stubbornness alone. 

Finally, after four years, I found a replacement sub-board online for cheap. I replaced it myself.

## Post-Repair Blues

The laptop worked again. For a while.

But the barrel charger started having issues. Loose connection. Partial contact. If you wiggled the cable *just right*, it charged. Otherwise, the battery drained while plugged in.

Static discharge became a regular occurrence. Touching the metal chassis would sometimes shut it down.

## The Lesson

Some repairs are worth doing yourself. Some aren't. The line between "savings" and "new problems" is thinner than you'd think.

That laptop taught me more about hardware troubleshooting than any textbook could. But I wouldn't wish a four-year repair saga on anyone.
