## What is Sustainable Development?

Sustainable development is the practice of using resources to meet our present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet theirs. As defined by the Brundtland Commission (1987):

> "Sustainable development is development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs."

## Why Do We Need It?

- **Declining air quality** — industrial emissions and vehicle pollution are at critical levels in many cities
- **Climate change** — rising global temperatures, extreme weather, and melting ice caps
- **Ecological imbalance** — deforestation, loss of biodiversity, and ocean acidification

Without sustainable practices, the very systems that support life on Earth are at risk.

## The 17 Sustainable Development Goals

The SDGs are a universal call to action end poverty, protect the planet, and ensure peace and prosperity by 2030:

1. No Poverty
2. Zero Hunger
3. Good Health and Well-being
4. Quality Education
5. Gender Equality
6. Clean Water and Sanitation
7. Affordable and Clean Energy
8. Decent Work and Economic Growth
9. Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure
10. Reduced Inequality
11. Sustainable Cities and Communities
12. Responsible Consumption and Production
13. Climate Action
14. Life Below Water
15. Life on Land
16. Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions
17. Partnerships for the Goals

## The Three Pillars of Sustainability

### Environmental Sustainability
Using resources at a rate that nature can replenish them. Focus on degradation, equilibrium, and renewal.

### Economic Sustainability
Moving beyond GDP as the sole measure of success. Edward Barbier (1987) argued for an economy that improves quality of life without depleting natural capital.

### Socio-political Sustainability
The World Bank (1999) linked genuine savings rates to long-term social stability. The IPCC Sixth Assessment Report (2022) reinforced that social equity and environmental policy are inseparable.

## Sustainable Cities

Urban planning with green spaces, solar-powered buildings, efficient public transport, and smart infrastructure. Cities consume over two-thirds of the world's energy and emit 70% of CO₂ — making urban sustainability critical.

## Education for Sustainable Development (ESD)

ESD, as defined by the UN and embedded in SDG Target 12.8, aims to equip learners with the knowledge, skills, values, and attitudes needed to create a more sustainable world. It's not just about teaching facts — it's about changing how we think.
